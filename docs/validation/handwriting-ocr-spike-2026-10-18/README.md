# Handwriting OCR spike, 2026-10-18/19

Classification: **SIMULATION_ONLY pilot.** It asks one question: can a
ready-made, pretrained OCR model read handwritten blood-label fields without
training a model from scratch? It is not Buno's OCR corpus, not an accuracy
claim, and changes nothing in the application. `INBOUND_OCR_V1`, the 90%
gate, the flags and `RQ-02` are unchanged.

## Data

- 24 fictional cards in a spike-only layout (`SPIKE_MEDIATRIX_LIKE_HANDWRITTEN`
  v1). It follows how the reference bag label is filled in: Collection Date,
  ABO/Rh Type, Donation No. and Expiry Date, each written below its heading,
  plus a Component box. It copies no branding, logo, screening sticker,
  manufacturer text or barcode. Values are invented: `MM26-MM-92NN`, `M-D-YY`
  dates and spoken blood types such as `B Positive`. They were generated by
  `scripts/ocr/handwriting-cards.mjs`.
- **Writer L** wrote all 24 cards on the printed layout, one phone photo per
  card (iPhone HEIC converted to JPEG).
- **Writer B** wrote 16 cards freehand on plain paper, with handwritten headings
  and deliberately varied styles (neat, cursive, rushed, upright), several cards
  per photo. Automatic line finding worked for 9 cards; 1 of those was
  misaligned and dropped, leaving 8 cards scored.
- **Answer key:** what is actually written, read by one reviewer. Three
  writer deviations were scored as written. One genuinely ambiguous field was
  excluded. **Pilot-grade:** there was no second independent reviewer.
- **Totals:** 159 scored fields from 2 writers (L 119, B 40).

Photos, crops, the answer key and predictions stay private in
`build/ocr-private/handwriting-spike-20261018/` and are not in Git.

## Method

1. Each handwritten value was cut out as a crop.
   - Printed cards: the box lines were detected, the top 30% of each box (the
     printed heading) was dropped, and the crop was trimmed to the ink.
   - Freehand pages: written lines were found and taken as alternating
     heading and value lines.
2. Every model read the same crops on this laptop's CPU, offline after a
   one-time model download.
3. Exact match after case and whitespace normalisation. Character error rate
   (CER) is capped at 1 per field.
4. **Valid-but-wrong** counts a read that passes the field's format check
   (`MMYY-MM-NNNN`, `M-D-YY`, the 8 blood types, the 5 component codes) but is
   wrong. That is the dangerous kind of error.

| Model (pretrained, unchanged) | Size | Runtime |
|---|---|---|
| Tesseract.js 7.0.0 `eng` LSTM, single-line mode (the app's current engine) | 4 MB | Node, 36–107 ms/field |
| TrOCR-small-handwritten (`Xenova/trocr-small-handwritten`, fp32) via `@huggingface/transformers` 4.3.0 | ~300 MB | Node/ONNX, ~0.5 s/field |
| PaddleOCR PP-OCRv5 mobile recogniser (`paddleocr` 3.0.3, `paddlex` 3.0.3, `paddlepaddle` 3.0.0) | 17 MB | Python container, ~0.23 s/field |
| PaddleOCR PP-OCRv5 server recogniser | 82 MB | Python container, ~0.41 s/field |

**Not run:** TrOCR-base-handwritten (fp32, 1.6 GB). Running it alongside the
other jobs exhausted this laptop's 3.7 GB WSL memory and stopped Docker
Desktop once. The stack recovered with no lost or stuck commands (ledger
height 1192).

## Results

| TrOCR-small-handwritten | 2/119 (2%) | 1/40 (3%) | 0/32 | 3 |

## Findings

1. **TrOCR-small is unsuitable without fine-tuning.** Trained on English
   sentences, it rewrites codes as words (a blood type read as an English
   word, a Donation No. as a name).
2. **Tesseract cannot read handwriting** (2–8% of fields). The app's
   printed-label path stays correct for printed labels only.
3. **PP-OCRv5 server is the only promising pretrained model.** It reads 83%
   of fields from the clearer writer and 57% from the other, with no
   training.
4. **Most errors are dangerous because they look valid.** Writer L's `6` was
   read as `4`, turning `26` into `24` in dates and Donation Nos. That
   confusion alone explains 26 of the 31 valid-but-wrong errors for that writer;
   the other 5 are also `6` misreads, as `0` or `0`/`4` together. A wrong year or Donation No. that
   passes the format check could bind the wrong unit.
5. **The engine's confidence does not separate right from wrong.** Wrong
   reads scored up to 99. With a ≥90 gate like the app's, 27 of 68 accepted
   fields from writer L were wrong. Even at ≥98, 3 of 10 were. Confidence
   cannot replace operator confirmation.
6. **Layout matters.** Fixed printed boxes cropped reliably, while freehand
   pages lost 8 of 16 cards to segmentation.

## Conclusion and next step

Ready-made OCR, used unchanged, does not read handwritten blood-label fields
reliably enough to record custody. The best model got 5 of 32 cards fully
correct, and its errors look valid. Handwritten capture therefore stays out of
the submittable path.

PP-OCRv5 server is the candidate to **fine-tune**, not to train from scratch,
on Buno's planned corpus: multiple writers, writer-disjoint splits and two
independent reviewers. Any future handwritten path still needs per-field
operator confirmation. Writer-specific confusions such as `6`/`4` also argue
for checks against the label grammar and for printed or barcode identifiers
where the hospital can provide them. `RQ-02` stays open.

Self-validation: one person acting for Jopia and Lat, with two writers.
